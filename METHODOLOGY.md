# Catalogue Methodology

This catalogue maps two related changes in work:

1. **AI-native roles** that primarily exist because organisations build, deploy, or govern AI systems.
2. **Evolving occupations** whose established responsibilities are being materially redesigned around AI delegation, verification, and oversight.

The unit of analysis is a responsibility bundle, not a fashionable title. Titles are recorded as market signals, but profiles group work that may appear under several names.

## Classification

Every profile is classified on four independent dimensions.

### Role Origin

- **Existing** — the occupation predates widespread AI adoption.
- **Hybrid** — an existing discipline has developed a distinct AI-specific specialism.
- **AI-native** — the responsibility bundle primarily exists because AI systems exist.

### Operating Model

- **AI-assisted** — AI tools are optional aids inside an otherwise familiar workflow.
- **AI-augmented** — AI performs material parts of the workflow, while people still coordinate most steps.
- **AI-first** — the workflow is designed around AI delegation, verification, and escalation.
- **Agent-supervised** — people oversee semi-autonomous systems, exceptions, and outcomes rather than executing each step directly.

Operating model is not a maturity scale. An AI-native role can still be AI-assisted, and an established occupation can become agent-supervised.

### Market Maturity

- **Emerging** — early public signals, inconsistent title, or work commonly bundled into another role.
- **Formalising** — repeated public hiring or organisational patterns, but scope still varies.
- **Established** — responsibilities, titles, and seniority bands recur across the market.

### Evidence Confidence

- **High** — at least two independent current primary signals support the responsibility bundle, with no material contradiction from the wider evidence.
- **Medium** — one current primary signal plus an independent supporting signal, or multiple signals whose titles or scope vary materially.
- **Early signal** — credible public evidence exists, but exact-title hiring or adoption is not yet recurrent.

Confidence measures evidence quality, not the importance or future potential of a role.

## Inclusion Rules

### AI-Native Roles

A role profile must show:

- a coherent responsibility bundle;
- at least one public signal;
- meaningful separation from adjacent roles; and
- identifiable work outputs, skills, and failure modes.

### Evolving Occupations

Routine use of an AI assistant is not enough. An occupation-evolution profile needs evidence of at least two of the following:

- material redistribution of tasks between people and AI;
- new verification, evaluation, or escalation duties;
- workflows designed around AI systems or agents;
- changed expectations for volume, quality, or cycle time;
- redistribution of entry-level, expert, or management responsibilities; or
- recurring adoption evidence across employers, professional bodies, or credible first-party case studies.

Profiles must distinguish observed practice from a proposed future operating model.

## Evidence Standard

Evidence is ranked as follows:

1. **Primary** — employer posting, official company description, regulator, standard, professional body, or public-sector guidance.
2. **Secondary** — analyst research, credible market study, or repeated recruiter descriptions.
3. **Early** — practitioner writing, founder/operator material, or a single niche description.

For every source, record:

- employer or publisher;
- advertised title or document title;
- evidence type and geography;
- live and archived URLs where available;
- date first observed and last verified;
- whether the source is active, closed, moved, unavailable, or historical; and
- the precise claim it supports.

Closed postings remain valid historical observations, but must not be described as current. A mutable URL whose content changes must be treated as a new observation.

## Scope and Sampling

The catalogue uses public English-language evidence across markets. It does not estimate prevalence or compare labour-market size. Geography is recorded because a role that recurs in one jurisdiction or sector should not be presented as universal.

Discovery is purposive rather than exhaustive. A review should record its search date, candidate titles, included sources, and rejected near-matches. Multiple postings from one employer count as one organisational signal unless they represent clearly separate functions.

## Review Cadence

- Check external source availability weekly through automation.
- Review active job-posting evidence at least monthly.
- Reassess profile classifications quarterly.
- Update `reviewed_at` only when a profile's evidence and classification have actually been reviewed.

Automated availability checks do not replace editorial review. Authentication walls, bot protection, and soft-error pages require a human status decision.

## Writing Rules

- Use “observed on” for historical job-posting evidence.
- Use “currently advertises” only when the source has recently been verified as active.
- Do not turn a proposed role name into an asserted market title.
- Identify which responsibilities are evidenced and which are synthesis.
- Preserve human accountability explicitly in AI-first and agent-supervised workflows.
